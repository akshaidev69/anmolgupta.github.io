CRM development cost in India (2026): custom CRM vs Zoho, Salesforce and HubSpot

Anmol Gupta · AI Engineer & Full Stack Developer
17 Nov 2026 · 8 min read

Most growing businesses in India reach the same point: leads are scattered across WhatsApp chats, Excel sheets, IndiaMART and JustDial enquiries, website forms and someone's notebook. Follow-ups get missed, and nobody knows which salesperson is handling which customer. The answer is a CRM. The real question is whether to subscribe to a ready CRM such as Zoho, Salesforce or HubSpot, or to get a custom CRM built for the way your team actually works.

This guide explains what drives custom CRM development cost in India in 2026, how it compares with subscription CRMs over three years, and how to get a fair quote. It is written for business owners and sales heads, not developers.

Short answer: what does a custom CRM cost in India?

A custom CRM in India is usually priced as a one-time build plus a small yearly cost for hosting and maintenance. A simple lead-management CRM for a small team costs far less than a multi-branch system with WhatsApp automation, accounting sync and a mobile app. The price depends mainly on the number of modules, integrations, user roles and reports, not on the number of users.

That last point matters. Subscription CRMs charge per user, per month. A custom CRM does not, so it becomes more attractive as your team grows.

What drives custom CRM development cost

1. Modules you need

Every module adds design, development and testing time. Common ones are:

  • Lead management: capture, assign, track status and follow-up dates
  • Contacts and accounts: customer history, notes, documents
  • Sales pipeline: deal stages, expected value, win and loss reasons
  • Quotations and invoices: GST-ready quotes and invoices generated as PDF
  • Tasks and reminders: calls, meetings, site visits, renewal dates
  • Support tickets or service requests: for after-sales teams
  • Reports and dashboards: conversions by source, salesperson, city or product

Start with the modules that directly affect revenue. Others can come in phase two.

2. Integrations

Integrations are where a custom CRM really earns its value in India, and they are also a major cost driver:

  • WhatsApp Business API for automatic lead replies, reminders and broadcasts
  • IndiaMART, JustDial, Meta lead ads and Google Ads lead sync, so enquiries land in the CRM automatically
  • Website forms and landing pages
  • Tally, Zoho Books or another accounting tool
  • Payment gateways such as Razorpay for payment links
  • Telephony / IVR for click-to-call and call logs
  • Email and SMS with DLT-compliant templates

Each integration depends on the third party's API, so ask your developer which ones are straightforward and which need extra work.

3. User roles and permissions

A two-person business needs simple logins. A company with branches, team leaders, telecallers and field staff needs role-based access: who can see which leads, who can edit prices, who can export data. More roles mean more rules to build and test.

4. Mobile access

Field sales teams need the CRM on their phone. A responsive web CRM works well in the mobile browser and costs less. A dedicated Android or iOS app adds features such as offline use, GPS check-ins and push notifications, but increases the budget. See mobile app development if your team works mostly in the field.

5. Data migration

Moving years of leads from Excel or an old CRM takes cleaning and mapping. Budget a little time for it, and ask for a test import before the final switch.

Custom CRM vs Zoho, Salesforce and HubSpot

Ready CRMs are excellent products. For many small teams they are the right choice. Here is an honest comparison.

When a subscription CRM makes sense

  • Your team is small and unlikely to grow fast
  • Your sales process is standard (lead, deal, invoice)
  • You want to start this week, not next month
  • You are comfortable adapting your process to the software

When a custom CRM makes more sense

  • Per-user fees are adding up. Multiply the per-user monthly price by your users and by 36 months. Compare that ₹ figure with a one-time build.
  • Your process is unique. Real estate site visits, education admissions, manufacturing dealer networks, clinic appointments and insurance renewals rarely fit a generic pipeline.
  • You need Indian integrations such as IndiaMART, Tally or WhatsApp, which are often paid add-ons or limited in ready CRMs.
  • You want to own your data and the software, without lock-in or price rises.
  • Your team finds the ready CRM too complex and simply stops using it.

A simple three-year cost check

  1. Note the plan you would actually need (not the cheapest one), with the add-ons for WhatsApp, automation and reports.
  2. Multiply by the number of users today and the number you expect in two years.
  3. Multiply by 36 months and add implementation or consultant fees.
  4. Compare with a custom quote: one-time build plus three years of hosting and maintenance.

If the numbers are close, choose the option your team will actually use every day.

How long does it take to build a CRM?

A focused lead-management CRM can go live in a few weeks. A larger system with several integrations, roles and a mobile app takes longer and is best delivered in phases. A phased approach also spreads the cost: the first version starts paying back while later modules are built.

Hidden costs to watch for

  • Hosting in the developer's name with a fee to transfer it later
  • No source code handover, which locks you to one vendor
  • WhatsApp API and SMS charges that are paid to the provider, separate from development
  • "Every change is extra" after the first version, with no agreed revision rounds
  • No backups or no plan for security updates

A good quote lists what is included, the recurring costs, who owns the code and server, and how support works after launch. Our guide on how to choose a web development company covers more red flags.

How to get an accurate CRM quote

  1. Map your current process from first enquiry to payment, even on paper.
  2. List your lead sources and the tools you already use.
  3. Count your users and the roles they play.
  4. Pick the three reports you most want to see every Monday.
  5. Ask for a demo or prototype before committing to the full build.
  6. Insist on milestone payments tied to working features.

Frequently asked questions

Is a custom CRM better than Zoho for a small business in India?

Not always. For a very small team with a standard process, Zoho or a similar CRM is often enough. A custom CRM becomes better value when per-user fees grow, when you need Indian integrations like IndiaMART, Tally or WhatsApp, or when your process does not fit a standard pipeline.

Can a custom CRM work with WhatsApp?

Yes. Through the official WhatsApp Business API, a CRM can send automatic replies to new leads, follow-up reminders, payment links and updates. Message charges are paid to the provider separately.

Will I own the CRM software?

You should. Make sure the contract says the source code, database and server account belong to you. I hand over full code and access on every project.

Do I pay per user for a custom CRM?

No. A custom CRM has no per-user licence. You pay for the build and for hosting and maintenance, so adding team members does not raise your monthly bill in the same way.

Can my old Excel data be moved into the new CRM?

Yes. Leads and customer data from Excel, Google Sheets or an older CRM can be cleaned and imported, ideally with a test import first.
