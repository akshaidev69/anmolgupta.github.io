Website development cost in India (2026): what really drives the price

Anmol Gupta · AI Engineer & Full Stack Developer
13 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

If you have asked three developers in India what a website costs, you have probably received three very different answers. One freelancer quotes a small amount, an agency quotes many times more, and a third asks a dozen questions before giving any number. None of them is necessarily wrong. "A website" can mean a single landing page or a full online store with payments, shipping rules and an admin team.

This guide explains what actually drives website development cost in India in 2026, which costs repeat every year, where hidden charges usually appear, and how to get a quote you can trust. It is written for business owners, not developers.

The five things that decide the price

1. The type of website

The biggest cost driver is what the website needs to do.

  • Landing page or one-page site: a single page that explains your offer and collects enquiries. Fastest and most affordable.
  • Business website: usually five to fifteen pages such as Home, About, Services, Projects, Blog and Contact. This is what most service businesses need.
  • E-commerce store: product catalog, cart, payments, shipping, tax, order emails and an admin to manage it all. More moving parts, more testing.
  • Web application or portal: logins, dashboards, bookings, user roles or custom workflows. This is closer to custom software development than a regular website.

2. Custom design or a ready template

A template-based site is quicker because the layout already exists. A custom design is planned around your business, your customers and your brand. Both are valid choices. The problem is only when a template site is sold to you as "fully custom". Ask directly which one you are getting.

3. Number of pages and amount of content

Every page needs layout, copy, images, mobile testing and SEO basics. Twenty service pages take far longer than five. If you can provide your own text and photos, the cost usually drops. If the developer needs to write content or source images, expect that to be priced in.

4. Features and integrations

Each feature adds design, development and testing time:

  • WhatsApp chat, call buttons and enquiry forms
  • Booking or appointment systems
  • Payment gateways, UPI and cash on delivery
  • Multi-language content
  • CRM, email marketing or Google Sheets integrations
  • Member logins or customer accounts

5. Who builds it

A solo freelancer, a small studio and a large agency have very different overheads. Agencies may offer project managers and larger teams; freelancers usually offer direct contact with the person doing the work. What matters more than size is process: a clear scope, fixed timeline, ownership of your accounts and support after launch. We cover this in detail in how to choose a web development company.

Costs that repeat every year

Many business owners only budget for the build and are surprised later. These costs recur:

  • Domain renewal: your website address, renewed yearly.
  • Hosting: where the website lives. Shared hosting is fine for most small sites; busy stores may need more.
  • SSL certificate: often free with good hosting, sometimes charged separately.
  • Premium plugins or themes: some need annual licences for updates.
  • Maintenance: updates, backups, security checks and small edits.
  • Business email: if you want addresses on your own domain.

Ask for these to be listed separately in your quote so you know exactly what you will pay after year one.

Where hidden costs usually appear

The most common complaint we hear from business owners is not the price itself but the surprises after agreeing to it. Watch out for:

  • "Every change is extra" after the first draft
  • Hosting or domain registered in the developer's name, with a fee to transfer it to you
  • Charges for handing over the source code or admin logins
  • Content upload, SEO setup or mobile optimisation treated as add-ons
  • Maintenance that suddenly becomes mandatory

A good quote states the number of revision rounds, what is included and what is not, and who owns each account.

How to get an honest quote

  1. Write down your goal. More enquiries? Online sales? Bookings? The goal decides the features.
  2. List the pages you think you need. Even a rough list helps.
  3. Share two or three websites you like and say what you like about them.
  4. Ask for an itemised quote with build cost, recurring costs and timeline in writing.
  5. Ask who will own the domain, hosting and code. The answer should be "you".
  6. Ask to see something before paying in full. A demo, a homepage design or a milestone plan.

Ways to reduce cost without hurting quality

  • Start with the pages that bring business and add the rest later.
  • Provide your own text and real photos of your work.
  • Use a proven platform such as WordPress where it fits, instead of building everything from scratch.
  • Skip features you are not sure you need; they can be added in phase two.
  • Pay in milestones so you only pay for progress you can see.

Is a cheap website a false saving?

Not always. A simple, well-built site can serve a small business for years. The risk with very cheap quotes is usually not the design but what is missing: slow loading on phones, no SEO basics, accounts you do not control, and no one to call when something breaks. Compare what is included, not just the number at the bottom.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • How many revision rounds are included?
  • Will the website be built on WordPress, a custom stack or something else, and why?
  • Who writes and uploads the content?
  • Is basic SEO (titles, meta descriptions, sitemap, Search Console) included?
  • How long is the free support period, and what does maintenance cost after it?
