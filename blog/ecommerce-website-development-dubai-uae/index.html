Ecommerce website development in Dubai and the UAE (2026): cost in AED, features and how to choose a developer

Anmol Gupta · AI Engineer & Full Stack Developer
22 Nov 2026 · 8 min read

Online shopping in the UAE is mobile-first, bilingual and fast-moving. Shoppers in Dubai and Abu Dhabi expect quick delivery, Arabic and English options, familiar payment methods and a checkout that works smoothly on a phone. If your store feels slow or foreign to them, they leave for Noon, Amazon.ae or a competitor's Instagram shop.

This guide explains what ecommerce website development in the UAE involves in 2026, what drives the cost in AED, which features matter most for local buyers, and how to choose the right developer, whether you are a Dubai boutique, an Abu Dhabi distributor or a brand selling across the GCC.

Short answer: what does a UAE ecommerce website need?

A UAE online store should be mobile-first, support English and Arabic (with right-to-left layout), accept local cards plus Apple Pay, offer cash on delivery and ideally buy-now-pay-later options like Tabby or Tamara, show prices in AED with 5% VAT handled correctly, and integrate with a local courier. On top of that it needs fast pages and solid SEO so customers can find it on Google.

Shopify, WooCommerce or custom: which platform fits a UAE business?

Shopify

Best for brands that want to launch quickly and keep maintenance low. Shopify has apps for Arabic translation, UAE payment gateways and couriers. You pay a monthly plan plus app fees, and deep customisation can get expensive.

WooCommerce (WordPress)

Good for businesses that want ownership and flexibility at a lower running cost. It works well with Arabic, local gateways and content-heavy SEO. It needs proper hosting, security updates and a developer who keeps plugins under control.

Custom ecommerce (for example Laravel or a headless setup)

Right for B2B ordering, multi-vendor marketplaces, complex pricing, subscriptions, or integration with an ERP, POS or warehouse system. It costs more upfront but removes platform limits and monthly app fees.

A simple rule: start on Shopify or WooCommerce unless your business process genuinely does not fit them. Many UAE brands move to custom only after they outgrow a platform.

What drives the cost of an ecommerce website in the UAE (AED)

Prices in Dubai vary widely between freelancers, local agencies and offshore teams, so compare scope, not just the headline quote. These are the main factors that move your budget in AED:

  • Platform choice: a theme-based Shopify or WooCommerce store costs far less than a fully custom build.
  • Number of products and categories: bulk uploads, variants (size, colour) and product data cleanup take time.
  • Bilingual setup: Arabic content, RTL layout testing and translated product data add real work.
  • Payment and shipping integrations: each gateway, BNPL provider and courier connection is a separate task.
  • Custom features: B2B pricing, wholesale portals, booking, subscriptions or a mobile app.
  • Design: a custom design costs more than adapting a premium theme.
  • Ongoing costs: hosting or platform plan, apps, domain, SSL, maintenance and marketing.

Ask every developer for a written list of deliverables and a clear split between the one-time build cost and monthly running costs. That is the only fair way to compare quotes in AED.

Must-have features for online stores in Dubai and Abu Dhabi

1. Arabic and English with proper RTL

Translation alone is not enough. Menus, filters, product pages and checkout must display correctly right to left, and Arabic fonts must stay readable on mobile. Separate language URLs also help Google show the right version to the right shopper.

2. Local payment methods

UAE shoppers commonly pay by Visa, Mastercard and Apple Pay. Popular gateway options include Telr, Network International (N-Genius), PayTabs and Checkout.com. Buy-now-pay-later services such as Tabby and Tamara can raise average order value for fashion, electronics and furniture.

3. Cash on delivery

COD is still expected by many UAE customers, especially for first orders. A good store lets you enable COD per product or emirate, add a COD fee if you choose, and confirm orders by WhatsApp or SMS to reduce fake orders.

4. VAT-ready pricing and invoices

The UAE charges 5% VAT. Your store should show whether prices include VAT, generate tax invoices with your TRN, and export order data cleanly for your accountant.

5. Courier and delivery integration

Same-day and next-day delivery are normal in Dubai. Integrating with your courier (for example Aramex or a local last-mile partner) lets you create shipments, print labels and send tracking links automatically, with delivery fees by emirate.

6. WhatsApp and social commerce

Many UAE purchases start on Instagram or WhatsApp. A floating WhatsApp button, product sharing links and a synced Instagram or Meta catalogue help turn social traffic into orders.

7. Speed on mobile

Most traffic arrives on phones. Compressed images, lazy loading, a lean theme and good hosting keep product pages fast, which helps both conversions and Google rankings.

8. Privacy and trust

Show clear return, delivery and privacy policies, use SSL everywhere, and handle customer data responsibly in line with the UAE personal data protection law. Trust badges and real reviews matter more than flashy design.

SEO for a UAE ecommerce store

A store nobody can find does not sell. Build SEO in from day one:

  • Unique product and category descriptions, not supplier copy pasted from other sites.
  • Category pages targeting local searches like "buy office chairs Dubai" or "abaya online UAE".
  • Product schema with price in AED, stock status and reviews.
  • Arabic pages optimised for Arabic keywords, not just machine-translated English.
  • A Google Business Profile if you have a showroom or pickup point.
  • Answer-first FAQ content so AI tools like ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews can cite your store.

How long does a UAE ecommerce build take?

A theme-based Shopify or WooCommerce store with a modest catalogue can usually launch within a few weeks once content and product data are ready. Bilingual stores with several integrations take longer, and custom marketplaces or B2B portals are typically multi-month projects. The biggest delay is almost always product data and Arabic content, so start preparing those early.

How to choose an ecommerce developer in the UAE

  1. Check live stores, not mockups. Open them on your phone and try the checkout flow.
  2. Ask about UAE specifics. They should talk confidently about Arabic RTL, COD, Tabby or Tamara, VAT and local couriers.
  3. Confirm ownership. Your domain, hosting, platform account and code should be in your name.
  4. Agree on support. Get the post-launch support period and monthly maintenance terms in writing.
  5. Consider remote specialists. Many Dubai businesses work with experienced developers in India for better value, with overlapping working hours (India is only 1.5 hours ahead of the UAE).

FAQ

How much does an ecommerce website cost in Dubai?

It depends on the platform, number of products, bilingual needs and integrations. A theme-based Shopify or WooCommerce store is the most affordable option, while custom marketplaces and B2B portals cost significantly more. Always compare quotes by their written scope in AED, including monthly running costs.

Is Shopify or WooCommerce better for the UAE?

Shopify is better if you want speed and low maintenance. WooCommerce is better if you want ownership, lower monthly fees and strong content SEO. Both support Arabic, local gateways and cash on delivery.

Do I need an Arabic version of my online store?

Not always, but it helps. Many UAE shoppers browse in English, yet an Arabic version widens your audience across the UAE and GCC and lets you rank for Arabic searches.

Can I offer cash on delivery and Tabby on the same store?

Yes. Most platforms let you offer cards, Apple Pay, COD and BNPL options like Tabby or Tamara together at checkout.

Do I need a trade licence to sell online in the UAE?

Generally yes, you need an appropriate licence that covers ecommerce activity, from a mainland authority or a free zone. Check the exact requirements with your licensing authority or business setup consultant.

Can a developer outside the UAE build my store?

Yes. Ecommerce development is done remotely all the time. Choose someone with live stores to show, clear communication and experience with UAE payments, couriers and Arabic.
