Buying guide

How to choose a web development company (and avoid getting cheated)

Anmol Gupta · Full Stack & App Developer, Aksh
12 Nov 2026 · 8 min read

Most business owners only hire a web developer once every few years, which makes it hard to know what "good" looks like. Unfortunately that also makes it easy to get burned. Stories of delayed projects, surprise bills and websites held hostage are common in every market, whether you hire in India, the USA or anywhere else.

This guide gives you a practical checklist: the red flags to watch for, the questions to ask, and what to get in writing before you pay. It applies to agencies and freelancers alike.

The most common ways clients get cheated

Understanding the usual problems makes them easy to spot early.

Hidden costs

The quote looks reasonable, then every small change, plugin or "server setup" becomes an extra charge. By launch, the bill is far higher than agreed.

The developer keeps your domain, hosting or code

Your domain or hosting is registered in the developer's account. When you want to move or stop paying them, you discover you cannot take your own website with you.

Ghosting after payment

Full payment up front, followed by slow replies or none at all. Without milestones there is little reason for the project to stay a priority.

Missed deadlines

"Next week" keeps moving. There is no written timeline, so there is nothing to hold anyone to.

Templates sold as custom work

A ready-made theme with your logo swapped in, priced as a bespoke design. Templates are fine; misrepresenting them is not.

No source code or admin access

You never receive the code or full admin access, so every future change has to go through the same person, at their price.

Fake SEO promises

"Guaranteed first page of Google in 30 days." No one controls Google's rankings, so a guarantee like this is a warning sign, not a selling point. Read our SEO checklist for small businesses to see what real SEO work looks like.

No support after launch

The site goes live, and help stops. Small fixes suddenly cost a lot.

Ten questions to ask before you hire

  1. Can I see live projects you built? Click through them on your phone. Do they load quickly? Are the contact buttons easy to find?
  2. Who will own the domain, hosting, code and logins? The only acceptable answer is you.
  3. Can I see a demo or design before paying in full?
  4. How are payments split? Milestones tied to visible progress are safer than one large advance.
  5. What exactly is included? Pages, revisions, content upload, mobile optimisation, basic SEO, analytics.
  6. What is not included? A good developer answers this clearly.
  7. What is the timeline, in writing? Including what you need to provide and by when.
  8. Is this a custom design or a template? Either can be fine. You just deserve to know.
  9. What happens after launch? Is there a free support period? What does maintenance cost after that?
  10. Who will I actually talk to? With agencies, ask whether you will speak with the designer or developer, or only a sales contact.

Red flags in the proposal

  • A price with no breakdown of what is included
  • No mention of who owns the domain and hosting
  • 100% advance payment
  • Unlimited promises with a very low price
  • Ranking or sales guarantees
  • Pressure to decide today
  • No written timeline or contract

Green flags

  • An itemised quote with build cost and recurring costs separated
  • A demo, wireframe or homepage design before full payment
  • Milestone payments
  • Accounts created in your name, with you as owner
  • Full source code and admin access handed over at launch
  • A clear support period after launch
  • Fast, honest communication, including saying "no" to things that are not worth your money

What to put in writing

Even a simple one-page agreement protects both sides. Make sure it covers:

  • Scope: pages, features and number of revision rounds
  • Timeline and milestones
  • Payment schedule tied to milestones
  • Ownership of domain, hosting, code, designs and content
  • Handover: source code, logins and a short walkthrough
  • Support period and maintenance rates after launch

Agency or freelancer?

Agencies can offer bigger teams and broader services. Freelancers often offer lower overheads and direct contact with the person building your project. Neither is automatically better. Judge them on their work, their process and whether they put the points above in writing. If you are comparing locations, our guide to app development cost in India vs USA covers how working across countries affects price and process.

How to check a portfolio properly

A portfolio page full of screenshots tells you very little. Live projects tell you much more. When you review a developer's work:

  • Open each project on your phone, not just a laptop. Most of your customers will.
  • Check the speed. Does the homepage appear quickly on mobile data?
  • Try the contact path. Can you find the call, WhatsApp or enquiry button within a few seconds?
  • Look for variety. Different industries and layouts suggest real design work rather than one template reused.
  • Ask what they did on each project. Design, development, content, hosting, marketing or all of it.

If possible, ask whether you can speak with a past client. Many developers will happily arrange it.
